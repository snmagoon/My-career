# Rs 90 lakh (PKR 9,000,000): monthly-income options, analysis as of 21 Oct 2026

Goal: earn a monthly profit you can withdraw and spend, while making it hard to touch the principal.

> Rates change often and many of the sources below are third-party blogs. Treat every number as indicative and confirm with the bank or National Savings Centre before you invest. Items I could not verify are marked **(unverified)**.

Main finding: **National Savings pays more than any bank monthly-profit product I found (12.12% vs about 10.75% at best), has no stated maximum, is sovereign-backed, and is exempt from zakat.** Banks matter for diversification, the liquid reserve, and the Sharia route.

## 2. National Savings: the core of the plan

### Regular Income Certificate (RIC), conventional
Source: savings.gov.pk/regular-income-certificates

- Tenure 5 years. Profit is paid **monthly** from the issue date.
- Rate 12.12% (Rs 1,010/month per Rs 100,000).
- Minimum Rs 50,000. **The official page states no maximum.**
- Denominations: 50,000; 100,000; 500,000; 1,000,000; 5,000,000; 10,000,000.
- Premature encashment is allowed any time, with a service charge on face value: **2% before year 1, 1.5% before year 2, 1% before year 3, 0.5% before year 4, nil after year 4.** This is your built-in "restriction".
- Exempt from zakat deduction.
- Tax: 15% for filers on the Active Taxpayer List, 30% for non-filers, withheld at source.
- Joint holding: Joint-A (both holders together) or Joint-B (either holder).
- Sold at National Savings Centres, authorised scheduled-bank branches and the SBP.
- Payment by cash or by cheque, draft or pay-order.
- Documents: CNIC copy and Form SC-1 (free). Overseas Pakistanis use NICOP or POC.
- Whether the rate is locked for the full 5 years on a certificate you already hold: not confirmed, so ask. **(unverified)**

Islamic bank products (HalalWallet, rates June-Aug 2026 and not all dated the same):
- **Meezan Certificate of Islamic Investment:** minimum Rs 50,000. 1-year monthly option 9.01%, 1.5-year monthly option 10.05%. Early payout follows an approved schedule. Islamic banks declare profit after month end.
- **BankIslami TDR:** 1-year Mahana Munafa 8.51%. A longer tenor shows higher but is unclear.

Premature withdrawal at banks varies. HBL pays the lowest of several rates, one being the last completed tenure's rate minus 1%. Bank AL Habib pays the last completed tenure's rate. Others: ask. Typical alternative: savings rate minus 1%.

## 4. Savings accounts
SBP's minimum deposit rate is about 10% for average balances up to Rs 10m, per Pakera. That is close to the term-deposit rates, but nothing locks the money, so it works as the emergency reserve and not as the restricted portion.

## 5. Tax (important, and I corrected an earlier figure)
- National Savings profit: 15% filer, 30% non-filer (official CDNS page).
- Bank deposit profit: reported as **20% filer, 40% non-filer**. Sources conflict and I could not confirm the final Finance Act 2026 rates. **(unverified)** My earlier answer assumed 15% for banks, which was too low.
- Being on the Active Taxpayer List matters a great deal. A non-filer loses roughly a third more of the income.
- The Finance Act 2026 also added Section 165AB, requiring banks to report accounts with more than Rs 100m of debits in six months. This is not relevant at your size.

## 6. Safety
- Deposit protection is Rs 1,000,000 per depositor per bank, so Rs 90 lakh in one bank is mostly unprotected. National Savings is sovereign-backed, and the official page sets no maximum on RIC.
- The bank deposit limit is the reason to keep at most Rs 10 lakh per bank (or a little more, accepting some risk) where you use banks.

## 7. Documents
- **National Savings (official):** CNIC copy and Form SC-1.
- **Banks (general practice, not confirmed from a bank's page):** CNIC, tax status/NTN, a recent photo, address proof, nominee details, and source-of-funds proof (sale deed, bank statement, salary slips) for a deposit this size. **(unverified)** Ask the branch for its checklist.

## 8. Recommended structure (assumes you are a filer)

| Bucket | Amount | Where | Rate used | Gross/month | Net/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locked core | Rs 60 lakh | RIC, or Sarwa Islamic Term Account if you need Sharia compliance | 12.12% | 60,600 | 51,510 (15% tax) |
| Bank diversification | Rs 20 lakh | Monthly-profit term deposits, Rs 10 lakh each at two banks (for example Bank AL Habib and JS Bank) | about 10.5% | 17,500 | 14,000 (20% tax) |
| Liquid reserve | Rs 10 lakh | Savings account, ideally at a different bank | about 10% | 8,333 | 6,667 (20% tax) |
| **Total** | **Rs 90 lakh** | | | **86,433** | **about 72,200** |

If you are a non-filer, the same plan yields roughly Rs 58,000 a month (30% on National Savings, 40% on banks, and a net of about Rs 42,400 + 10,500 + 5,000). Filing first is worth it.

Alternative with the highest income: everything in RIC gives Rs 90,900 gross, about Rs 77,300 net (filer). I don't recommend it because you would have no liquid reserve and a single issuer.

### Tips to make the restriction stick
- Buy several certificates, not one. For example, split Rs 60 lakh as 5m + 1m, so an emergency means breaking one certificate and not all of them.
- Have the monthly profit credited to a separate account and spend only from that.
- Use a joint holding (Joint-A, so both holders must act) if you want a second person's agreement before capital can move.
- Stagger the bank deposits so maturities fall in different months.

## 9. Questions to confirm with the branch
1. Is the RIC profit rate fixed for the full 5 years for a certificate bought today?
2. What is the maximum a single branch will issue in one transaction, and does it need prior notice for large amounts?
3. For each bank: profit payment date, premature-withdrawal formula, and whether the rate is fixed.
4. Final 2026 tax rates on bank profit for your filer status.
