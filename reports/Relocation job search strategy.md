# Target sponsor registers, not Easy Apply buttons

**Bottom line.** Saif's salary band (EUR 72-100K) clears the legal pay floor for a skilled-worker permit in almost every researched country, so employer willingness to sponsor decides the outcome, not salary eligibility. The one exception is the Netherlands, where the 2026 floor is about EUR 71.3K a year *before* 8% holiday pay, so an offer has to be at least that figure in base pay ([Grant Thornton NL](https://www.grantthornton.nl/insights/human-capital-services/wijzigingen-salarisdrempels-2026/)). The best-evidenced plan is to run a sponsor-register-first search in Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland and the UK, and to treat Denmark, Sweden, Luxembourg, Australia, Singapore and Canada as a second wave. Gulf states are a conditional side-channel, because the Saudi work-visa position for Pakistanis is unconfirmed. Interviews should come mainly from three sources: employers confirmed as sponsors, referrals and direct recruiter contact, and tailored direct applications. Referrals are the strongest-evidenced channel, at about 40% vs 3% interview rates in an Ashby dataset as reported by secondary sources ([Dataford](https://dataford.io/blog/do-referrals-help-2026)). Bulk Easy Apply and LinkedIn automation add account-ban risk, and no reliable source shows they produce interviews ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). This report rests almost entirely on search snippets and law-firm, recruiter and aggregator pages. The research notes opened almost no official government page, and several figures conflict. Section 6 lists what to verify before acting on any figure here.

### Tier 1: Germany, Netherlands, Ireland, UK (apply first, most volume)

Germany and Ireland combine very low permit floors (EUR 45.9-50.7K and EUR 40.9K) with salary ranges that bracket the target band, so a normal senior offer is automatically eligible. The Netherlands pays in band (senior average about EUR 73.8K, range EUR 55-110K) and has a public register of recognised sponsors, which is the single most useful filter in the research ([ElyForma](https://elyforma.com/blog/netherlands-highly-skilled-migrant-visa)). Its weakness is that the floor is almost exactly Saif's floor, and a .NET-with-Azure sample averaged only EUR 61.4K, so the AI-architect framing matters here. The UK is Tier 1 for volume and for its register, which UKVI updated 196 times in 2026 through 2 November ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers)). It is Tier 1 for getting a job but weaker for staying: the proposed "earned settlement" model sets a 10-year baseline for standard Skilled Workers and is not yet in force, and most Immigration Salary List entries are due for removal on 31 December 2026 ([Magrath](https://www.magrath.co.uk/resources/news/uk-immigration-mid-year-review-2026-for-businesses/), [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/uk/uk-immigration-law-key-changes-in-2025-and-what-to-expect-in-2026)). Whether Saif's goal is a job, long-term residence or both should decide how much weight the UK gets. I found no UK salary data, so UK pay realism is untested.

The ranking of these four against each other rests on thresholds, pay ranges and register availability. It does not rest on any evidence about how often these countries' employers sponsor Pakistani applicants or on interview rates for Pakistan-based candidates, and I found none. Germany and the Netherlands are named as common sponsorship markets only in marketing-oriented guides ([JobRise](https://jobrise.io/en/blog/how-to-get-visa-sponsorship-tech-jobs-2026/)). English-language prevalence, which matters for a candidate without local languages, was not measured for any country.

### Tier 2: Denmark, Sweden, Luxembourg, Australia, Singapore, Canada (run in parallel at lower volume)

Denmark's DKK 552,000 floor (about EUR 74K) means only offers near the top of Saif's band qualify, and only base pay, employer pension and holiday allowance count, not bonuses ([Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/denmark-foreign-labour-new-salary-thresholds-under-the-pay-limit-schemes-and-updated-positive-lists/)). Denmark also runs a Fast-track Scheme on the same salary track. Sweden's threshold is trivial against his floor, and Sweden has processed Pakistan-based applications since April 2025 ([Envoy Global](https://www.envoyglobal.com/news-alert/sweden-resumes-residence-and-work-permit-processing-in-pakistan/)). Extension applications from 2 December 2026 move to the 90% rule, which does not affect a first application ([Migrationsverket](https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/employers/news-archive-for-employers/news/2026-06-01-new-rules-for-work-permits-come-into-force.html)). Luxembourg's EUR 65,652 Blue Card floor leaves little headroom for offers at the bottom of his band, and a senior average of about EUR 69K means many offers will fall in the awkward zone between floor and target. Two sources, Fragomen's snippet at EUR 71,946 and a comparison site at EUR 80,640, conflict with the law-firm figure, so confirm the figure before applying.

Australia, Singapore and Canada have clear sponsored routes and pay near target, which is why the Anglosphere note ranks Singapore and Australia first among the non-European options. All pay conversions in that note use an unsourced exchange-rate assumption. Australia's Core Skills threshold of AUD 79,499 is far below his floor, but the Core Skills Occupation List and skills-assessment rules were not checked. Singapore's COMPASS points test is the main hurdle and was not researched. Canada's Global Talent Stream wage rules were not found, and the 2026-28 Levels Plan cuts temporary-resident arrivals sharply (about 230,000 new workers, a 37% drop), so the employer must be willing to run an LMIA ([UP Immigration](https://www.upimmigration.ca/blog/canada-immigration-levels-plan-2026-2028-targets)).

### Tier 3: opportunistic only (apply when a specific sponsor-confirmed role appears)

Belgium, Austria, Finland, Norway, Czechia, New Zealand, Spain, Portugal, Malta, Switzerland and France belong here. Belgium and Austria clear their thresholds, but their senior averages (about EUR 69K and EUR 68.5K) sit at or below his floor. France's all-engineer average of about EUR 62.8K is below it. Switzerland pays far above his floor but is limited by a hard quota of 4,500 B permits, and reported quota use conflicts (about 74% by end-2024 vs about 52% by end-September 2025) ([Vischer](https://www.vischer.com/en/knowledge/blog/quotas-for-foreigners-in-switzerland-in-2026/)). A Swiss employer must usually prove no suitable EU/EEA candidate exists, but that is my background knowledge, not a retrieved source. Spain, Portugal and Malta have fast-track routes (Spain HQP, Portugal Tech Visa, Malta Key Employee Initiative at about EUR 45K), but senior .NET averages found for Portugal (about EUR 31.9K) and Poland make EUR 72K a top-of-market outcome there ([Payscale PT](https://www.payscale.com/research/PT/Job=Senior_.NET_Developer_%2F_Programmer/Salary)). Finland (EUR 3,937/month) and Norway (NOK 545-625K) clear on thresholds, but I found no pay or demand data for them. Norway's processing runs through a consulate in Pakistan, and NOKUT degree evaluation can take 2-3 months ([VisaAtlas](https://visaatlas.org/from/pakistani/to/norway/skilled-worker-permit)). Romania, Hungary, Italy, Greece and Cyprus were not researched at all.

### Tier 4: Gulf (ex-UAE), Malaysia, Hong Kong (deprioritise, with conditions)

The Gulf is the most uncertain region. Hays reports strong GCC demand for technical and digital specialists, with 66% of employers growing headcount in 2025 ([Zawya, Hays release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/hays-middle-east-releases-hays-gcc-salary-guide-2026-369616)). But 93% of Saudi employers employ Saudi nationals and 75% plan to add more, a localisation headwind. Saudi cloud-architect pay estimates cluster around SAR 300-400K a year, which is about USD 80-107K tax-free (SAR pegged at 3.75/USD) ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/SA/Job=Cloud_Solutions_Architect/Salary)). The notes also record a reported Saudi suspension of temporary work visas for 17 nationalities including Pakistan (February 2026), against a Jobbatical statement (updated 1 September 2026) that there is no nationality-based ban, only possible quota pauses and delays of 4-12 weeks ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/saudi-arabia-temporary-suspension-of-temporary-work-visas-for-select-nationals.html), [Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/saudi-visa-nationality-restrictions)). Fragomen's page returned 403, so I only saw a snippet. Nothing was found for Qatar, Oman or Bahrain visa processing. Malaysia was moved down because local senior .NET pay (about RM 114K, roughly EUR 23K) is far below target, and Hong Kong because local pay data is thin and Pakistan treatment is unconfirmed.

## Sponsor registers beat platform filters, and referrals beat both

No platform's "visa sponsorship" filter was verified, and the notes found no reliable evidence that any of them works well. Where a public register exists, use it first. The UK register lists licensed sponsors and the Netherlands has a public IND recognised-sponsor list. The Dutch rule that the legal employer on the contract must match the register entry matters, because some groups register one entity and offer from another ([ElyForma](https://elyforma.com/blog/netherlands-highly-skilled-migrant-visa)). Ireland's critical-skills route waives the labour market needs test for listed occupations, which suggests multinationals and consultancies in Dublin, but no sponsor-volume data was retrieved ([Expert Consultants](https://expertconsultants.ie/critical-skills-occupations-list/)). For Germany, there is no register in the notes, so sponsor-friendliness has to be judged from the posting itself ("visa sponsorship", "relocation support") and from recruiter contact.

The workflow is: download the register, shortlist employers by city and sector, check their careers pages for .NET/Azure/AI architect roles, then apply on the employer's own site and approach an engineer or recruiter there for a referral. Referrals are about 7% of applicants but 30-50% of hires in an aggregated estimate, and the Ashby 40% vs 3% interview figure comes from secondary reports of an unread original ([Dataford](https://dataford.io/blog/do-referrals-help-2026)). Direct vs Easy Apply numbers from vendor blogs range from 1-3% to 15-30% and are mutually inconsistent, so I give them no weight beyond "direct is probably better". A Huntr-attributed comparison puts Google Jobs responses at 11.29% vs LinkedIn at 3.10%, but the methodology is not stated, so treat it as a weak hint to use Google Jobs as a discovery layer, not as a rate to plan around ([bestjobsearchapps](https://bestjobsearchapps.com/articles/en/best-job-sites-for-easy-applications-in-2026-high-response-rates-from-600k-apps)).

Regional site routing, from what the notes support: for Germany, Make-it-in-Germany (federal portal), LinkedIn, StepStone, Xing and Arbeitnow (visa-sponsorship tags) ([Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/service/newsletter-no-4-/-2026), [Arbeitnow](https://www.arbeitnow.com)). For curated relocation leads, Relocate.me's weekly "Global Move" list, which claimed 160 jobs in one 2026 edition (publisher's claim; the $12/month paid tier is unconfirmed on a primary page) ([Relocate.me](https://relocate.me/companies-hiring/the-global-move)). For the Gulf, Bayt, foundit Gulf, Hays and Michael Page ([Bayt](https://www.bayt.com/en/saudi-arabia/jobs/it-jobs/)). For the connectors, Indeed and Workable are the natural fit for Europe-wide title searches by country, and Dice and ZipRecruiter appear in the brief but I found no evidence on their reach outside North America. I would give them a two-week trial and drop them if they return no sponsor-friendly non-US roles. That is a judgment, not a finding. Landing.jobs and Honeypot were acquired (2025 and 2019), and Jobbatical's status is unclear, so do not rely on them ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/honeypot-vs-landingjobs)).

**Automation and terms-of-service risk.** LinkedIn's help page prohibits bots, browser plug-ins and extensions that scrape or automate activity under User Agreement Section 8.2, with account restriction as the stated consequence ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). Reported ban rates for auto-apply tools range from "mostly temporary Easy Apply pauses" to 70-85% (both low-confidence vendor claims). Indeed's automation position was not retrieved. The cautious reading: use Claude via connectors and Google search for discovery and for drafting, and keep any Claude-in-Chrome activity on employer career sites, one application at a time with Saif reviewing. Do not drive LinkedIn Easy Apply or messaging through Chrome automation. Saif's LinkedIn profile is probably the single most valuable asset in a referral-led plan, and losing it would cost more than any application gain. Remote-from-Karachi roles through employer-of-record platforms exist (Remote, Deel and others state they cover Pakistan, per vendor pages), but they provide income, not a visa, and which employers hire in Pakistan is unverified ([Remote](https://remote.com/ko-kr/country-explorer/pakistan/employer-of-record)). Treat them as a bridge, not part of this plan.

## Weekly plan: fewer applications, aimed at interview conversion

I found no data on interview rates for sponsorship-seeking or Pakistan-based candidates, so the volumes below are my planning judgment, anchored on weak numbers: roughly 3-5% direct-application response (low-confidence vendor claims) and much higher for referrals. They are a hypothesis to test over four to six weeks, not a forecast. At 12 tailored direct applications and 6 warm outreaches a week, direct applications would yield about 0.4-0.6 responses a week at those rates, and outreach should add more if even a fraction of the Ashby referral effect holds for a cold-contacted referrer. Expect first conversations in roughly 3-6 weeks and revisit the plan if there are none after about 40 applications. Market conditions favour the senior profile: Indeed data cited by secondary sources show 71% of the May 2025 to May 2026 rise in software-development postings came from senior roles, and AI-titled roles were 37% of the rise, although postings remain about a quarter below February 2020 ([TekRecruiter](https://www.tekrecruiter.com/post/software-engineering-hiring-trends)). The December slowdown and January hiring restart are general knowledge, not sourced in the notes. Use December to prepare and build referral relationships, and apply hardest from the second week of January.

| Day | Activity | Volume | Output measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Refresh sponsor-register shortlists (UK, NL) and new Workable/Indeed results for Tier 1-2 countries; Claude produces a ranked list with JD fit notes | 40-60 postings screened | 15-20 shortlisted |
| Tue | Full JD read, tailor, apply on employer sites for the best-fit roles in Germany/Netherlands/Ireland | 4-5 applications | Logged applications |
| Wed | Referral and recruiter outreach: 6 personalised messages to engineers, hiring managers or in-house recruiters at shortlisted employers, sent by Saif from his own LinkedIn | 6 messages | Replies, call bookings |
| Thu | Applications for UK, Denmark/Sweden/Luxembourg and one Tier 2 non-European target | 4-5 applications | Logged applications |
| Fri | Follow-ups (one per application after 7-10 days), interview prep, log review, adjust queries | 5-8 follow-ups | Updated tracker |
| Weekend (30 min) | Tier 3 and Gulf check: one or two sponsor-confirmed roles only | 0-2 applications | |

Metrics to track weekly are applications sent, replies, screening calls, hiring-manager interviews, and the source of each (register-filtered, referral, direct, Easy Apply). If after four weeks the register-filtered and referral sources produce all the replies, shift volume toward them. If a country produces no replies from 10 applications, pause it and re-check whether the roles really sponsor.

## Recommended changes to Saif's existing rules

**Skills filters.** Keep the exclusions, but make them conditional on how the skill appears. Treat Java, PHP, Go, Python, Kubernetes, AWS and Terraform as disqualifying only when stated as required or as the primary stack; treat "nice to have", "preferred" or "familiarity with" as acceptable. The reason is that cloud-engineer postings carry Azure 46%, Terraform 38% and Kubernetes 32%, whereas across all US tech postings Kubernetes is 14% and Terraform 8%, so a hard filter on any mention would discard many Azure architect roles ([CompareTheCloud summary](https://www.comparethecloud.net/news/cloud-platforms-command-us-tech-hiring-as-aws-leads-one-in-three-job-postings), [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.io/press-area/tech-tools-us-job-postings-demand)). Python is the dominant skill in AI postings (with LangChain and LLM integration), so a blanket Python exclusion removes much of the AI market ([Tredence](https://tredence.com/blog/ai-engineer-salary-in-2026-complete-guide)). Python-centred roles should still be skipped, while roles that list Python as one of several languages should be flagged for Saif's decision. I found no evidence on what share of AI roles require Kubernetes, AWS or Terraform.

**Experience filter.** The "skip 10+ years" rule is reasonable but should only trigger on a stated mandatory minimum of 10 or more years. Ranges like "8-12 years" or "8+ years" are fine. This is my judgment, as no source measures how rigidly years are enforced for senior roles. The senior share of postings (69.3% in Q1 2026, per a secondary source) supports applying to senior roles with a modestly wide experience range ([maketocreate](https://maketocreate.com/?p=1643)).

**Positioning.** Lead with "AI/agentic solution architect on Azure" (Azure OpenAI, RAG, agents), and keep .NET as the enterprise-integration credential. The notes suggest this beats a generic .NET pitch: Dutch .NET-with-Azure pay averaged EUR 61.4K, below the senior-engineer average, and AI/MLOps specialists earn a premium in Germany ([Ravio](https://ravio.com/blog/software-engineer-salary-trends)). Frame project management as AI-programme delivery, because coordination-type roles look most exposed to layoffs and the notes' evidence on TPM demand is mixed ([Mind the Product](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/what-the-data-tells-us-about-tech-and-pm-lay-offs-in-2026/)). The notes also suggest adding Python fluency and Terraform/AKS literacy, but that is advice to Saif about skills he may choose to acquire. Claude must never claim a skill that Saif's resume does not support.

**Salary floor, net vs gross for the Gulf.** Keep EUR 72K gross as the European floor, but note the Netherlands needs a base of at least EUR 71.3K excluding holiday pay, and Denmark at least DKK 552,000 counting pension. For the Gulf, a gross-for-gross comparison overstates Europe, because European figures are before income tax and social contributions while Qatar and Bahrain pay is described as tax-free ([Lifeindexed Qatar](https://lifeindexed.com/salary/middle-east/qatar/software-engineer/)). I found no sourced net conversion. As a working assumption, which Saif should confirm with a tax calculator, accept a tax-free Gulf package with total monthly cash (salary plus housing and transport allowances) worth at least the net equivalent of EUR 72K in his target European country. Also require visa and flights paid by the employer, and treat any recruiter fee request as a scam signal. The Gulf pay evidence is thin (Payscale, aggregators, one unverified EY Riyadh figure), so no Gulf role should be accepted or resigned for without a written offer and HR confirmation of the visa position.

**Cover letter.** Use a short, role-specific note of about 120-180 words that names the employer's actual stack from the JD, cites one or two relevant things from Saif's resume, and states relocation readiness and a one-month notice in a single sentence. For employers confirmed on a sponsor register, one line saying he needs sponsorship and meets the permit threshold saves everyone time. No source measures tailoring effectiveness, so this is judgment. Never invent personal narrative; if a form asks for motivation or a story, stop and ask Saif.

**Resume choice.** Use the AI/agentic-architect version for AI, GenAI and Azure architect roles, the .NET architect/tech-lead version for enterprise, banking and consultancy roles, and the delivery/PMP-forward version only for programme or delivery-lead roles. Claude should pick from Saif's existing resumes and must not rewrite facts. Where there is no good match, it should say so and ask. Before listing certifications, verify them on Microsoft Learn: one LinkedIn post claims AZ-204, AI-102 and others retire between June and September 2026, while another page still lists AI-102 (weak sources) ([CertDemand](https://certdemand.com/certs/ai-102)). Certifications look like a screening signal rather than a pay lever.

**Pacing.** Replace "as many applications as possible" with the weekly plan above: about 8-12 tailored applications and about 6 warm outreaches a week. Add a 7-10 day follow-up rule. Add three dated reminders: the Dutch and Danish thresholds will be re-indexed in January, so recheck them; the UK shortage-list changes on 31 December 2026; Singapore's EP minimum rises on 1 January 2027.

## Confidence and gaps: what to verify on official sites before acting

The research notes are search-snippet based and mostly secondary: law-firm alerts, immigration consultancies, recruiter blogs, aggregators and vendor content. Their method notes state that official government pages were generally not opened. The Western Europe note did not fetch any official page. The Anglosphere note fetched only three full pages (Baker McKenzie on Malaysia and Australia, and the New Zealand immigration page). The Gulf note had Fragomen and Gulf Business return 403. The numbers for pay come from Payscale, LifeIndexed, Levels.fyi, JobRise and similar sources, which mix base and total compensation, self-reported and posted pay, and have small samples. Payscale tends to read lower and Levels.fyi higher. Confidence is highest for the Dutch, Luxembourg, Australian and Swedish thresholds, because law-firm and agency pages agree. It is lowest for the Gulf, Poland, the Baltics, Portugal and the pay and demand claims of the sections on hiring and channels.

**Conflicts left unresolved.** Germany: one source says Federal Employment Agency approval is not needed at EUR 50,700, another says it is needed at the reduced threshold, and another source gives a general threshold of EUR 45,300. Netherlands: some pages still show 2025 figures (EUR 5,688), and one blog's reduced figure of EUR 4,754 is treated as an error. UK: general threshold quoted as GBP 41,700 by most sources and GBP 38,700 by one, and Immigration Salary List treatment differs by source. Luxembourg: EUR 65,652 (law firms) vs EUR 71,946 (Fragomen snippet) vs EUR 80,640 (comparison site). France: shortage-role threshold of about EUR 47.5K is unverified. Belgium: Brussels EUR 51,551 does not match the arithmetic and is treated as an error. Austria: shortage-route rules and IT's status conflict. Switzerland: quota use of 74% vs 52% in different periods. Sweden: threshold SEK 34,470 (new) vs SEK 33,390 (outdated). Estonia, Lithuania, Portugal and Spain thresholds each conflict across secondary sources. Malta: EUR 45K vs older 30-35K. Canada: Global Talent Stream processing times conflict across sources. New Zealand: it is unclear whether ICT pay rules tied to the median wage still apply. Malaysia: duration caps and Category III figures disagree. Layoff totals differ widely, so use them only as "heavy but not collapsing" context.

**The Saudi suspension is unconfirmed.** The report of 17 nationalities including Pakistan comes from a snippet of Fragomen's page and a second law firm, with no formal announcement or duration. The May 2025 pause for 14 countries around Hajj was described as temporary. Jobbatical (1 September 2026) says there is no nationality ban. Nothing in the notes confirms status as of November 2026. Ask the employer or recruiter in writing before investing effort, and do not resign or commit to a start date without a signed offer and HR confirmation.

**What to verify before acting, by priority:**

1. For any country where an offer will be pursued, the official pages: gov.uk (Skilled Worker), enterprise.gov.ie (Critical Skills), IND (recognised sponsors and kennismigrant thresholds), Make it in Germany or BAMF (Blue Card, and whether Federal Employment Agency approval applies), Migrationsverket, SIRI (Denmark), Migri, UDI, plus IRCC/ESDC, Home Affairs and MOM for the Anglosphere targets.
2. Whether his BSc counts as a "relevant degree" for Ireland's route (probably yes, but not checked against the official list), and any degree-recognition or attestation steps (NOKUT for Norway; HEC and embassy attestation for the Gulf, the latter unsourced).
3. The UK sponsor register file itself, the Dutch IND register and whether each target employer's offering entity matches; whether the Immigration Salary List still covers software roles after 31 December 2026; and the Dutch and Danish January 2027 re-indexing.
4. Processing times and Pakistan-specific consular steps, which the notes did not retrieve for most countries (Sweden resumed processing in Pakistan in April 2025; Norway files through a consulate; the 2023 Schengen short-stay approval rate for Pakistanis was 47.1%, which is a short-stay figure and not a work-permit rate) ([VisaProcessInfos](https://www.visaprocessinfos.com/nationality/pakistani/sweden)).
5. Family reunification and permanent residence rules, which were not retrieved for any country. The UK 10-year settlement proposal is the one concrete finding.
6. Net-pay comparisons: no tax or cost-of-living data was retrieved for any country, so the Gulf-versus-Europe comparison is unquantified.

8. Platform terms: Indeed's, Workable's, Dice's and ZipRecruiter's automation rules were not retrieved, and the visa/relocation filters on all job boards were not verified. Read each platform's terms before any Claude-in-Chrome use there.

## Conclusion

The main change this research suggests is a shift from "which country pays enough" to "which employer can and will sponsor". On the available evidence the salary floor is rarely binding, since the Netherlands and Denmark are the near-misses, and the open question is employer behaviour, on which the notes hold almost no data. That is why the plan puts public sponsor registers and referrals ahead of site filters and Easy Apply, and why the instruction set makes sponsor evidence a logged field for every application. It also means the plan is a testable hypothesis: four to six weeks of tracked results by source and country will tell Saif more about Pakistan-based sponsorship odds than anything in the current notes.

A second implication is that several of his current rules cost him real options. Hard bans on Python, Kubernetes, AWS and Terraform discard roles where those skills are optional, and a gross-for-gross Gulf comparison overvalues Europe's headline salary. The countries that look strongest on thresholds and pay (Germany, Ireland, the Netherlands) are also those where the notes are thinnest on sponsorship rates, English-language prevalence and processing times for Pakistani applicants. Closing those gaps on official sites is a few hours of work and should come before the first application.
